# Primal Directive — $3,000 MRR Go-to-Market Plan

**Owner's goal:** reach $3,000 in monthly recurring revenue (MRR) without abandoning the existing Primal Directive app, repo, or production project.

**Decision:** sell a measurable business outcome to working independent bands, not a generic “AI for musicians” bundle. The first wedge is **booking follow-through + show operations + post-show money reconciliation**. Expand to broader band operations only after the wedge is trusted and used.

## 1. The revenue math

MRR is recurring subscription revenue, not profit, cash collected from setup fees, or a forecast of what the product will earn.

| Average paid subscription | Paying band workspaces needed for $3,000 MRR |
|---:|---:|
| $49/month | 62 |
| $79/month | 38 |
| $99/month | 31 |
| $129/month | 24 |
| $149/month | 21 |
| $199/month | 16 |

**Recommended validation target:** 25 active band workspaces at an average of $129/month = $3,225 MRR before payment processing, hosting, support, refunds, taxes, and churn. This is a target, not a promise. Do not optimize for the headline number at the expense of retention or product reliability.

### Pricing hypothesis to test—not publish as final until interviews

- **Founding Band — $79/month:** one band workspace, shared gig/venue pipeline, task packs, show calendar, basic money tracking, merch stock/sales, exports, and guided onboarding. Limited founding cohort; pricing may be grandfathered only if the economics remain viable.
- **Band Operations — $129/month:** everything in Founding plus full show lifecycle, deposit/balance follow-up tracking, post-show reconciliation, show-level actual-versus-estimated margin, recurring workflow rules, and collaborative member tasks.
- **Manager / Multi-band — $199+/month:** multiple band workspaces, cross-band overview, reusable templates, and consolidated reporting. Validate demand and seat/workspace costs before launch.
- **Optional setup service:** a one-time paid import/configuration session may help early cash flow, but it is not MRR and must not be counted toward the $3,000 recurring goal.

Do not sell a tier based on external automation until its integrations are connected, authorized, monitored, and verified end-to-end. Until then, sell what actually works: organized records, internal task automation, clear workflows, and transparent manual approval steps.

Competitors establish a low-price floor for basic band organization. Artist Growth positions itself toward creator teams and uses a sales/demo path. Public product pages are not a complete feature audit, and competitor prices/features can change.

**Implication:** a $129–$199 price cannot be justified by a prettier calendar, a chatbot, or an “all-in-one” claim. It must be justified by saved labor and recovered value: fewer forgotten follow-ups, fewer missed deadlines, faster show closeout, clearer cash owed, fewer inventory surprises, and better decisions about which gigs are actually profitable.

## 3. The initial customer—not “every musician”

Start with working regional and independent bands that:
- play regularly and have recurring booking/advancing/merch work;
- have a member or manager currently coordinating operations in messages, spreadsheets, calendars, and memory;
- have enough activity that one missed follow-up, uncollected balance, or administrative hour matters;
- can name the person who owns the workflow and can pay for the workspace.

Do not start by targeting every hobbyist, solo beginner, major-label act, venue, promoter, and manager simultaneously. A narrow first customer makes product decisions and sales messages testable.

## 4. The offer: sell the outcome

**Proposed positioning:** “Primal Directive keeps every show moving—from booking follow-up to show-day tasks to final money reconciliation—so your band stops losing opportunities and spending nights doing admin.”

Lead with three outcomes:
1. **Nothing slips:** each qualified booking and confirmed show gets an owner, due dates, and visible next action.
2. **Know the real number:** distinguish quoted fees, expected payments, collected cash, expenses, merch sales, and actual show contribution.
3. **Close the loop:** after every show, reconcile what happened and use the verified result to improve future booking and routing choices.

Avoid claims like “runs your band entirely by itself,” “guarantees more bookings,” “recovers $X,” or “best in the market” until evidence supports them. A transparent product that completes a smaller workflow is more valuable than a grand promise that breaks.

## 5. 30-day customer validation and acquisition sprint

### Days 1–7: interview before overbuilding
- Build a list of 40 highly relevant bands/managers from public band pages, local/regional bills, venue calendars, and professional referrals.
- Conduct 10 short interviews. Ask them to walk through the last show from initial offer to final settlement; ask where details were lost, how many tools they used, and what they currently pay for.
- Request a recent anonymized workflow example or blank spreadsheet/template—not private financial data.
- Show a clickable/current product walkthrough only after confirming it works; label any incomplete integrations.
- Success gate: at least 6 of 10 describe the same painful workflow and at least 3 agree to test a concrete workflow. If not, revise the wedge before adding features.

### Days 8–14: concierge pilot
- Recruit 5 design partners. Do not pretend the software performs actions that are still manual.
- Help each import a small set of real upcoming shows and create task packs.
- Measure baseline admin time, overdue tasks, time to close a show, and whether balances/expenses are reconciled.
- Ask each partner to complete one full show workflow and one post-show review.
- Charge a clearly disclosed founding price only when the current delivered value justifies it; collect explicit feedback and cancellation reasons.

### Days 15–30: convert proof into paid workspaces
- Ask satisfied pilots to become paying customers and request one introduction each.
- Publish two specific case studies only with permission and verified before/after measurements.
- Reach out personally to 10 qualified prospects per weekday; personalize around a real public signal (touring frequency, upcoming dates, or management setup), not fake familiarity.
- Offer a short workflow audit and demonstration—not a vague “AI demo.”
- Keep a simple pipeline: identified → contacted → replied → discovery → pilot → paid → retained.
- Weekly review: qualified conversations, pilot activation, paid conversion, weekly active bands, task completion, support time, and churn risk.

The sprint is a learning and validation target, not a guarantee that 25 customers will arrive in 30 days.

## 6. Funnel math: assumptions to replace with real data

An illustrative funnel to reach 25 paid workspaces might require:
- 200 carefully selected prospects contacted;
- 40 meaningful replies/conversations (20%);
- 20 qualified demos (50% of conversations);
- 10 pilots (50% of demos);
- 5 paid customers (50% of pilots).

That yields only five customers from 200 contacts. Repeat and improve the process based on actual conversion; do not assume these percentages will hold. If 25 customers are the goal, the likely requirement is multiple prospect batches, referrals, partner channels, and retained pilots—not one viral post.

## 7. Distribution channels in priority order

1. **Founder-led direct outreach:** fastest feedback loop; target bands with upcoming shows and active calendars.
2. **Peer referrals:** ask each successful band for one introduction to a band with similar operational pain.
3. **Local/regional music ecosystem:** rehearsal studios, independent promoters, small booking agencies, music shops, and regional festivals. Offer a useful workflow session, not a broad sponsorship ask.
4. **Band managers and multi-band operators:** potentially higher account value, but only after multi-workspace permissions and reporting are reliable.
5. **Useful public content:** share practical templates such as a show-advance checklist, post-show settlement sheet, merch reconciliation sheet, and booking follow-up tracker. Each should demonstrate the product's actual workflow and point to a real demo/contact path.

Do not buy ads until interviews and paid pilots demonstrate retention and a repeatable message. Paid acquisition can amplify a working funnel; it rarely fixes an unclear offer.

## 8. Product requirements that directly support willingness to pay

Prioritize the following over broad feature sprawl:
- **Fast onboarding:** create a band profile, import gigs/contacts, choose timezone/currency, and see an honest empty state or clearly labeled demo.
- **Trustworthy data:** data persists across sessions/devices as designed; export/backup is available; no demo data contaminates real records.
- **A complete show lifecycle:** booking status, terms, deposit/balance, advancing, member tasks, promotion approval, merch pack, actual income/costs, reconciliation, and post-show review share canonical records.
- **Evidence-backed booking CRM:** each venue lead has a source URL, last-verified date, fit rationale, contact confidence, next step, and outreach status. Never invent contact details or imply a draft was sent.
- **Cash clarity:** keep quoted, contracted, outstanding, collected, and forecast amounts distinct. Link each number to its source record.
- **Action receipts:** each automation shows what it tried, when, what evidence confirms success, and how to retry safely. External side effects require provider confirmation.
- **Mobile-first reliability:** clear loading, validation, empty, offline/failure, and retry states; safe export; accessible controls.
- **Visible limits:** show which integrations are connected, what permission is granted, and what still requires human action.

## 9. Product north-star and launch gates

North-star: **verified admin hours saved per active band per week**, while missed deadlines, incorrect payments, and loss of control do not increase.

Track:
- time-to-first-value after signup;
- percentage of bands that add a real show and complete its task pack;
- weekly active paid workspaces;
- shows reconciled within 72 hours after the event;
- overdue balances identified and actually collected (reported separately);
- booking follow-up completion and reply rate;
- estimated-versus-actual show margin error;
- automation success, duplicate side effects, and manual correction rate;
- 30/60/90-day retention, cancellation reasons, and support hours per account.

**Do not launch a paid “autopilot” claim until** the correct production deployment is verified, authenticated data persistence is tested across sessions/devices, core flows pass end-to-end tests, external integrations are actually connected, and failures are observable/recoverable. A quota or deployment error is a release blocker, not a marketing footnote.

## 10. Explicit go/no-go rules

- If users praise the idea but won't pay, the offer or customer segment is wrong; don't interpret compliments as validation.
- If users pay but do not complete onboarding, fix time-to-value before adding features.
- If bands use only calendar/tasks but ignore money/reconciliation, test whether the core value should be narrower.
- If concierge service is the only reason they stay, price the service honestly and determine what can be productized.
- If users require a feature that depends on a third-party integration, validate access, provider terms, costs, and reliability before promising a ship date.
- If retention is weak, do not scale acquisition spend.
- If the app cannot reliably preserve data or complete the promised workflow, stop selling the affected promise and fix reliability.
